Programs

Connecting repositories, technology, research, and people

Long-term conservation capacity emerges when physical resources, methods, infrastructure, and trained teams advance together.

A connected system

Four program areas, one preservation mission

Each area addresses a different barrier to making aquatic genetic-resource conservation repeatable and sustainable.

01

Repository network

Strengthen the practices and relationships needed to steward preserved material across time, organizations, and intended uses.

Program focus

  • Repository planning and operating practices
  • Sample identity, metadata, and quality control
  • Storage, access, verification, and stewardship concepts
  • Coordination among collection, processing, and repository teams
02

Technology development

Create practical tools and workflow improvements that make preservation work more consistent, accessible, and scalable.

Program focus

  • Open and adaptable hardware concepts
  • Workflow fixtures, devices, and fabrication
  • Standardization and throughput improvements
  • Design for field and partner-facility realities
03

Research partnerships

Structure applied studies around real conservation needs, biological constraints, and decisions that partners must make.

Program focus

  • Protocol development and optimization
  • Validation and performance evaluation
  • Species- and material-specific questions
  • Methods, documentation, and knowledge exchange
04

Education and outreach

Help practitioners understand the complete preservation system and build the confidence to operate it responsibly.

Program focus

  • Hands-on technical training
  • Program and facility planning
  • Practical guides and learning resources
  • Communication across scientific and operational teams

Partnership model

Programs begin with a shared problem—not a predefined package

The strongest collaborations define the conservation objective, the partner’s operating context, the decisions the work must support, and what success will look like.

A useful first conversation includesSpecies or taxon, biological material, existing methods, project stage, participating organizations, available samples, desired timeline, and the capability the partner wants to build.
