Our story

Three decades of work, built into a national resource

The National Aquatic Germplasm Center was established in 2025 to collect, cryopreserve, and curate germplasm across the breadth of aquatic species—and to help communities develop repository capacity of their own.

That mission grows from more than 30 years of practical work across species, laboratories, hatcheries, agencies, and international partnerships.

Operating principles

Conservation resources must be trusted to be useful

Quality before volume

Scale matters only when identity, viability, repeatability, and documentation remain defensible.

Fit the biology

Methods should respond to the organism and biological material, not force every project into one template.

Design for adoption

Tools and protocols must work in the facilities, field settings, and staffing realities where they will be used.

Plan for stewardship

Ownership, access, metadata, storage, and future use should be addressed before material enters a repository.

Built through collaboration

Repository capacity grows through people

NAGC connects technical expertise, practical tools, training, and partner institutions so preservation capability can be sustained where it is needed.
