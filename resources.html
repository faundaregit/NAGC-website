01 · Project scoping brief

Describe the conservation problem before prescribing the solution

A concise project brief allows NAGC to identify feasibility questions, missing information, and the most useful next discussion.

  • Species, population, strain, or taxonomic scope
  • Biological material to be collected or preserved
  • Conservation, research, breeding, restoration, or backup objective
  • Approximate number of individuals, samples, collections, or storage units
  • Collection location, season, maturity, and handling constraints
  • Existing protocols, data, equipment, facilities, and trained personnel
  • Quality criteria or future-use requirements
  • Participating organizations, approvals, and decision makers
  • Desired milestones, timeline, and known budget constraints

02 · Sample submission planning

Resolve acceptance, identity, and transport questions before collection

Do not ship biological material until NAGC has confirmed acceptance, timing, packaging, documentation, and receiving arrangements for the project.

  • Confirm that the species and material are within the agreed project scope
  • Confirm collection, animal-care, transport, import, export, and other applicable approvals
  • Agree on identifiers, labels, metadata fields, and chain-of-custody needs
  • Document collection time, condition, handling, and any pre-shipment assessment
  • Confirm temperature, packaging, shipping service, timing, and contingency plans
  • Identify the sender, receiving contact, and communication procedure
  • Include only documentation approved for shipment with the material
  • Retain a project copy of manifests and sample records

03 · Partner onboarding framework

Make roles and stewardship expectations explicit

Collaborative projects move more efficiently when scientific, operational, and institutional responsibilities are understood at the beginning.

  • Name scientific, operational, contracting, and communication contacts
  • Define which organization supplies animals, samples, equipment, facilities, and personnel
  • Clarify approvals, material transfer, data use, confidentiality, and publication expectations
  • Define deliverables, review points, acceptance criteria, and change-control decisions
  • Agree on metadata, records, storage, access, ownership, and future-use terms
  • Plan training, transfer of methods, documentation, and post-project support
  • Identify schedule dependencies, risks, and escalation contacts

Frequently asked questions

Questions to resolve early

Can NAGC work with a species that is not shown on the website?

Potentially. The species page presents representative examples rather than a complete inventory. Feasibility depends on the organism, material, objective, sample access, and technical evidence available.

Should samples be shipped before a consultation?

No. Acceptance, schedule, packaging, documentation, receiving contacts, and applicable approvals should be confirmed before biological material is sent.

How is final pricing determined?

Guide prices support early planning. Final fees depend on the agreed scope, sample volume, technical uncertainty, deliverables, travel, materials, schedule, and stewardship requirements.

How are ownership, access, and data handled?

These terms should be defined in the written project agreement before work begins. Requirements vary by partner, material, funding, approvals, and intended future use.

What makes a first inquiry useful?

Include the species, biological material, project stage, conservation objective, approximate scale, location, existing methods, participating organizations, and desired timeline.
