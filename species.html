Experience across more than 200 species

Aquatic germplasm work spans different reproductive strategies, sample types, environments, and conservation objectives. The biology determines the pathway.

Species-specific by design

No single method fits aquatic diversity

Protocol selection and development must account for the organism, biological material, maturity, collection setting, available sample volume, and intended use after preservation.

What changes by project

The species is only the beginning

Scoping also considers the material, the decision the preserved resource must support, and the conditions under which work will occur.

01

Biological material

Sperm, embryos, tissues, cells, or other germplasm each introduce different collection and preservation constraints.

02

Project purpose

Conservation, research, breeding, restoration, backup, and technology development require different evidence and planning.

03

Operating setting

Field collections, hatcheries, laboratories, and distributed programs shape equipment and workflow choices.

04

Future use

Storage, access, quality criteria, metadata, and verification should reflect how material may be used later.
